From published studies

  • People look at the spot they are about to touch before the hand arrives, and don't look at the hand itself. Johansson et al., 2001
  • When an object is heavier than expected, the corrective force increase starts within about 90 to 100 ms. Johansson & Westling, 1988; McGarity-Shipley et al., 2023
  • Gaze-guided foveation cut a robot policy's vision tokens from 324 to 20 and made it more robust to distractors. Chuang et al., 2025
  • Error-related brain potentials can be detected in single EEG trials during robot interaction. Kim et al., 2017

Simulated on this page

  • The scene, the forces and the EEG trace are generated. Nothing here is recorded.
  • Lift-off 250 ms after contact, a memory that absorbs 60% of each error, and the saliency baseline are toy settings.
  • Token counts follow the 324 versus 20 comparison above. The share of tokens on the apple, gripper and plate is computed from this scene's geometry.
